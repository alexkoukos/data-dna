Strictly confidential. For the Board of Directors of Aegean Meridian Consulting only. Subject: expansion into the Balkans.

The management team recommends that the company opens offices in Sofia and Belgrade during the second quarter of 2027. The plan assumes an initial investment of four million euros, financed entirely from retained earnings, with break even expected after twenty two months. We do not plan to raise debt or to bring in outside investors for this move.

The fastest route into Bulgaria is to acquire a small Sofia firm, Danube Advisory, for one and a half million euros. The purchase gives us an existing client base in banking and telecommunications, eleven consultants who already work in our methodology, and a licence to deliver regulatory reporting work for Bulgarian banks. Danube Advisory earned two hundred and ten thousand euros before tax last year, and the founders have agreed to stay for at least three years.

In Serbia we do not intend to buy anything. We will rent an office in Belgrade, hire a country lead by the end of March, and staff the first projects with consultants moved from Athens and Thessaloniki. The first anchor client would be a telecommunications operator with whom we have already held two preliminary meetings. We expect the Belgrade office to reach twelve people by the end of its first year.

The main risks are the following. First, currency exposure: the Serbian dinar and the Bulgarian lev behave differently against the euro, and our contracts in both countries will be priced in euros but paid in local currency in some cases. Second, hiring: senior consultants in Belgrade are scarce, and we expect to pay a premium of fifteen percent over local market rates for the first two years. Third, competition: our largest competitor has already approached two of the same acquisition targets and may make a higher offer for Danube Advisory before we close. Fourth, key person risk: the Sofia firm depends heavily on its two founders.

Until the board has approved this plan, nobody outside the executive committee may discuss the acquisition, the budget or the timeline, internally or externally. In particular the Danube Advisory offer must not be mentioned to our banks, to our auditors or to any client. If the plan leaks, the sellers could withdraw or the price could rise.

The board vote is scheduled for the March meeting. A final decision on the Danube Advisory offer must be made before the fifteenth of April, because the sellers have given us an exclusivity period that ends on that date. If the board approves, we will announce the expansion to all staff in the first week of May, and the Sofia office will open on the first of July.

The executive committee asks the board to approve three things: the four million euro budget, the acquisition of Danube Advisory at up to one and a half million euros, and the appointment of the chief operating officer as sponsor of the programme.
